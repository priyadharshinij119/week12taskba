1. Key Findings
 1 — Spending is highly concentrated in Electronics and Software
Electronics is the largest spending category, followed by Software. Together, these two categories account for a major portion of the total expenditure. This shows that most of the organization's purchasing budget is focused on technology-related products and services.

 2 — Laptop and software license purchases contribute significantly to total spending
Laptops are the highest-spending individual item, followed by Annual Software Licenses. These two items contribute a large share of the overall spending, indicating that high-value technology purchases have a major impact on the organization's budget.

 3 — Spending varies across suppliers and time periods
The analysis shows that spending is not evenly distributed among suppliers or throughout the year. Some suppliers receive considerably more business than others, while certain months have noticeably higher spending. This indicates opportunities for better purchasing planning and supplier management.

 Recommendations:
1 — Negotiate better prices for high-value purchases
Since Electronics and Software account for a large portion of total spending, the organization should negotiate bulk discounts, better software licensing plans, and long-term contracts for frequently purchased technology products. This could help reduce overall procurement costs.

 2 — Improve supplier and purchase planning
The organization should regularly compare supplier prices and performance before making major purchases. Historical spending patterns can also be used to plan purchases during high-spending periods and avoid unnecessary or unplanned expenditure.


Conclusion:
The analysis shows that the organization's spending is mainly driven by technology-related purchases, particularly Electronics and Software. A small number of high-value items and suppliers account for a significant portion of the total expenditure. Spending also varies across different periods, showing that purchasing activity is not consistent throughout the year. Overall, the organization can improve cost control by focusing on high-value purchases, negotiating with major suppliers, and using historical spending patterns for better procurement planning.
